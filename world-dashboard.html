What this is

An animated scatterplot in the tradition of Gapminder: each bubble is one of 217 economies. The horizontal axis is income per person, the vertical axis is life expectancy, and bubble area is population. Press play and the world moves — countries drift up and to the right as they get richer and healthier, while a few move down and left during wars, epidemics and economic collapses.

Data

  • All series come from the World Bank, World Development Indicators, retrieved 6 November 2026 from the World Bank open API (api.worldbank.org).
  • GDP per capita, PPP — NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.CD, current international $, 1990–2024.
  • GDP per capita — NY.GDP.PCAP.KD, constant 2015 US$, 1960–2024. This one is not PPP-adjusted, so poor countries sit lower than in the PPP view; use it to see the longer history.
  • Life expectancy at birth — SP.DYN.LE00.IN. Population — SP.POP.TOTL. Fertility — SP.DYN.TFRT.IN. Under-5 mortality — SH.DYN.MORT. Urban share — SP.URB.TOTL.IN.ZS. Energy use per capita — EG.USE.PCAP.KG.OE (1990–2024).
  • CO₂ per capita — EN.GHG.CO2.PC.CE.AR5, emissions excluding land-use change, 1970–2024.
  • Aggregates (World, regions, income groups) are excluded; only individual economies are plotted. Small states with no data in any series are dropped.

2025 and 2026

  • Observations run to 2025 for GDP per capita, population and urban share, and to 2024 for life expectancy, CO₂, fertility, under-5 mortality and energy use. The World Bank publishes nothing for 2026 in any of these series (the API was last refreshed on 8 October 2026).
  • To reach 2026 the dashboard extends each country's own recent trend: the average annual change over its last ten observations, carried forward and clamped to a plausible range. Those years are drawn with an amber PROJECTED badge, an amber year watermark, and an ≈ before the value in the tooltip.
  • Switch “to 2026” off in the control bar to see observations only — the timeline then stops at the last real year for the selected indicators (2024 for the default view, 2025 if you plot GDP against population or urban share).
  • Projections are a visualisation aid, not a forecast: no model, no uncertainty band, no allowance for shocks.

How it is built

  • Axis domains are fixed across all years for the chosen indicators, so horizontal drift genuinely means "richer", not "the axis moved".
  • Missing years inside a country's series are linearly interpolated when the gap is 8 years or shorter; longer gaps stay empty and the country is simply not drawn in those years.
  • The timeline starts at the earliest year for which the selected indicators all have data (1990 for the default PPP view, 1960 once you pick the constant-dollar GDP series).
  • Headline numbers are population-weighted, so they describe people rather than countries.
  • Everything runs in this single HTML file — no libraries, no network calls, no tracking.

Reading the numbers

  • Click a bubble to pin a country. Pinned countries keep a label, a coloured 22-year trail and a full-history sparkline in the side panel.
  • Colour-by-indicator uses a log ramp (where the indicator is skewed) spanning the 1st–99th percentile, so a single outlier such as Palau's 200 t of CO₂ cannot wash out the palette.
  • Bubble area — not radius — is proportional to the size indicator.
  • space plays and pauses, ←/→ step one year, shift+arrow jumps five, and the timeline slider can be scrubbed.

Caveats

  • PPP conversion factors are estimates, and a log axis compresses large absolute differences between very poor and very rich countries.
  • CO₂ is territorial production only; it excludes emissions embedded in traded goods.
  • A few country-years contain implausible one-year jumps in the WDI life-expectancy series (for example the Central African Republic in 2009, 2019 and 2022). They are plotted exactly as published — no smoothing — so they appear as brief spikes.
  • 2025 values are the latest available in WDI and are subject to revision; 2024 is the latest for the demographic and emissions series.
